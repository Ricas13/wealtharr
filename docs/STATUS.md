The market worker accepts a quote that `assessQuote` rejected as IMPLAUSIBLE_MOVE only when an administrator-CONFIRMED, non-revoked `corporate_actions` row for the instrument has: an effective date not after the quote's UTC date, the quote within 14 days of it, a reference (the latest ACCEPTED observation of that line strictly before the effective date 00:00 UTC), a price within 5% of reference x from/to, and no already-adopted post-split price (the action has not already explained an observation of the line, and no accepted observation on/after the effective date is within 5% of reference x from/to, so a drift 100 -> 50 -> 30 followed by a 50 print is not explained again). The quote is stored linked to the action, the rejection cleared, and the run notes `CORPORATE_ACTION_EXPLAINED_QUOTE`. **Creating an action** needs two-step sign-in always, a rate limit, an https source, a note, and an OBSERVED quote that the ratio explains (a current rejection under the worker's own predicate, or an accepted post-split quote that matches); power-of-ten ratios additionally need an explicit confirmation and a unit/currency note; every admin is notified. **Two-person approval:** decided by numeric closeness, not the reduced form. With r = larger term / smaller term, a ratio with r >= 5 (either direction) or any reduced term above 50 is only PROPOSED (`PENDING`) and becomes `CONFIRMED` when a DIFFERENT two-step-verified admin approves it; so 2:21, 3:31, 5:49, 2:19 and their inverses (dropped-decimal errors in a split's clothing) need two people as well as 10:1, 100:1 and 1:100. A ratio with r within 15% of 10, 100 or 1000 (r in [8.5,11.5], [85,115], [850,1150]) additionally needs the explicit unit/currency confirmation and a 40-character note naming the check. Ordinary ratios (r < 5, terms <= 50: 2:1, 3:2, 4:3, 1:2 and so on) are confirmed by their creator. A creator cannot approve their own proposal, a proposal lapses to `REVOKED` after 7 days, either admin can reject it with a reason. **OWNER DECISION REQUIRED: add a second admin account or accept manual prices for such splits.** On an installation with a single admin account these proposals stay `PENDING` and lapse (fail closed), so common genuine reverse splits (1:5, 1:10) are not explained and holders rely on manually entered prices. There is NO in-product way to add a second admin: first-run setup (`src/lib/setup.ts`) only creates or promotes an admin while none exists, and the admin Users page is read-only for roles. If the owner wants a second admin, the operator must set `role='ADMIN'` on a second verified account directly in the database (and that person must enrol two-step sign-in before the console admits them); nothing here fabricates or assumes that sign-off. Only `CONFIRMED` rows are ever read by the worker and the holder guard. Other ratios are confirmed by their creator. **Revoking** deletes, in one transaction with the audit event, the observations the action explained and the later observations of those lines judged plausible against them, and restores a `CORPORATE_ACTION_REVOKED` rejection row for each affected line, so the previous accepted price is the reference again however old it is (the 7-day reference window does not apply while a rejection is unresolved): the next quote is accepted only if it is plausible against that reference or a new action is confirmed, and holders are `DATA_REQUIRED` meanwhile. Valuation snapshots (`performance_series` `USER_VALUE` rows, written only for the current day and not rebuildable) are deleted only for strategies holding the instrument at the effective instant or afterwards and only from the effective date forward, in one statement; a strategy that sold out before the effective date keeps its history. The deleted days show as gaps rather than invented returns. **Strategies:** per account and per action, units held at the effective date 00:00 UTC (rows reversed by corrections excluded) must be zero, or the account needs an unreversed SPLIT of the same ratio dated from effective_date-1d until the next confirmed action; otherwise valuation and sizing are `DATA_REQUIRED`. No manual price, reconciliation (cash only) or opening snapshot (adds units) clears it. Once recorded, a price observed before the effective date is never used with those units (`DATA_REQUIRED` until a post-split price arrives). 3:2 and similar small splits pass plausibility without any rejection, so holders are valued at the post-split price until an admin confirms the action; confirmation applies the same holder rule. **Recording the split after post-split trades:** with a CONFIRMED action, a user may record a split dated in [effective date - 1 day, effective date 00:00 UTC] with exactly its ratio even when later entries exist, provided every later entry is dated on/after that instant; units are restated from those held as of the instant (10 pre-split + 4 bought post-split becomes 24, not 28), and a later entry dated before it still refuses. The generic later-activity check ignores corrected trades and their correction rows. **Honest limits:** nothing is automatic; two colluding or compromised admins can confirm any action the observed-evidence rule allows, while a single-admin installation cannot confirm any ratio with r >= 5 (only ratios below 5, still bound by the observed-quote evidence and 5% band, are single-admin); effective dates are UTC dates, so exchanges whose session crosses UTC midnight (for example NZX) are out of scope until exchange-timezone support exists; `CORPORATE_ACTION` notifications use the ordinary notification delivery path (only `REVIEW_UPCOMING` is special-cased there), carry only instrument name, ratio and date, and are subject to each admin's plan channels; development or staging databases that applied an earlier version of migration 0057 (unmerged, since changed) must drop `corporate_actions` and the `explained_by_corporate_action_id` column and re-run it; no real corporate-action feed exercised; tests written but not run in this environment (no Node) |

- **Customer-visible strategy without sign-off (superseded).** At the time 9Sig was the only enabled, published strategy without a spec card. Since then the 9Sig release is no longer seeded on real installs or offered to customers, and sign-off became optional audit metadata (2026-10-12).
- **Fixed from the audit:** worker starvation by permanently failing strategies, notification head-of-line blocking by one failing provider, negative cash/holdings via corrections or withdrawals, a corrected fill leaving the review unexecutable, fabricated returns from backdated flows, future-dated ledger entries, annualised short-history community statistic, atomic/rate-limited email verification, strict Discord webhook URL parsing, error text stored in worker records, scheduler/backup container privileges.
- **Open hardening:** the app and backup connect to PostgreSQL as the superuser owner role; corporate actions are not modelled (large moves fail closed and need reconciliation); proration is Stripe's, not computed locally.
- **Not verified anywhere yet:** real Stripe, email, Telegram, Discord, market-data, FX, Oracle host, off-site restore, UK legal review, independent security review.

## Advance review reminder (optional)
- New setting `REVIEW_REMINDER_LEAD_DAYS` (Admin > Settings > Notifications, 0-14; blank = default 2 days, 0 = off). When set, the hourly worker creates one `REVIEW_UPCOMING` notification per strategy review ("<Strategy> review due <date>") for active strategies whose newest action is NO_ACTION with a fresh calculation, held until 08:00 in the user timezone, using the existing `review_key` unique index for one-per-review dedupe. Date only: no amounts or instructions. Channels follow plan entitlements and connected endpoints; a reminder is cancelled at send time (`REMINDER_NO_LONGER_CURRENT`) and hidden from the Activity page if the strategy is no longer active or the review date moved or has arrived. Known limits: the lead time is a multiple of 24 hours (not calendar days, so it can shift by an hour over a daylight-saving change); only strategies whose latest calculation is under 2 days old and says nothing is due get a reminder; a reminder already sitting in an inbox or chat cannot be recalled once sent. Migration 0036 adds nullable `strategy_instance_id` and `reminder_due_at` to `notifications`.
- Evidence: `tests/review-reminder.test.ts` (window, quiet hours, DST, key, wording, wiring), `tests/db/review-reminder.test.ts` (once per review, paused/closed/stale, plan channels, cancellation). Not yet run in CI at the time of writing.

## Admin entitlement super-user
- An ADMIN is an entitlement super-user: no active-strategy limit, every plan feature, every notification channel, no strategy-key restriction (`adminEntitlementSnapshot()` in `src/domain/entitlements.ts`, resolved by `loadEntitlementsWith` in `src/lib/entitlement-service.ts`). This is an entitlement bypass only; admin-only powers stay behind `requireAdmin` and MFA.
- Every plan-resolution path (create, reactivate, switch, linked accounts, enforcement, notifications, previews) goes through that loader, so the hourly enforcement pass never pauses an admin. Demoting an admin re-applies normal plan limits on the next enforcement pass (cron, plan edit or billing event). Tests: `tests/admin-entitlements.test.ts`, `tests/db/admin-entitlements.test.ts` (database test needs `DATABASE_URL`).
- An ADMIN can also start, switch to and preview a strategy whose definition is **disabled** for customers, to test it (`includeDisabledStrategies` on the entitlement snapshot, true only for a live ADMIN, derived server-side from the database role and never from request input, query string or token). The strategy must still have a PUBLISHED in-window version (a DRAFT is never startable, so an instance cannot be pinned to a mutable version) and still passes the market/mapping availability check. Customers never see or start disabled strategies; the new-strategy page labels these for admins as "Admin preview - not available to customers". Such instances are marked `strategy_instances.admin_disabled_preview` (migration 0035); if the owner is later demoted, `enforceStrategyEntitlements` pauses exactly those instances while the definition is still disabled and reactivation is refused. Customers who run a strategy that was disabled after they started it are unaffected. Tests: `tests/admin-entitlements.test.ts`, `tests/db/admin-disabled-strategies.test.ts`. Not yet executed: no Node toolchain was available when this was written, so these tests and the migration have not been run.

## Database integrity guards (migrations 0038 and 0039)
- **Released strategy versions are frozen.** `strategy_versions_guard_published()` (replaces the 0014 body) covers PUBLISHED *and* RETIRED rows: `config`, `input_schema`, `engine_key`, `effective_from`, `disclosure`, `version` and `strategy_definition_id` cannot change; the only lifecycle move away from a released version is PUBLISHED -> RETIRED (RETIRED is terminal; PUBLISHED/RETIRED -> DRAFT is refused with `STRATEGY_VERSION_LIFECYCLE_TRANSITION_REFUSED`). `effective_to`, `release_notes` and `upgrade_policy` stay editable. A new BEFORE DELETE trigger refuses deleting a released version that any instance, state, action, version migration, published track record or benchmark uses (`PUBLISHED_STRATEGY_VERSION_CANNOT_BE_DELETED`); unused throwaway versions and drafts remain deletable. Maintenance can still opt in per session with `SET app.allow_published_edit = 'on'` (never from the seed or the app).
- **How to add a strategy version safely:** insert it as a DRAFT (admin API or seed), edit freely while DRAFT, ATTEST (spec card + golden tests), PUBLISH, and to change rules create the next version and RETIRE the old one. Never edit a released row in place and never write a migration that updates a released version's rules: add a new version instead.
- **Attestations survive account deletion.** `strategy_version_attestations.attested_by` is now nullable with `ON DELETE SET NULL`, and `attested_by_email` keeps a snapshot (filled by the API and, as a fallback, by a trigger). Purging an admin who attested no longer fails with 23503.
- **Ledger/action references.** Unique `ledger_events_correction_unique` (one reversal per event; falls back to a plain index with a NOTICE if legacy duplicates exist), `ledger_events_instrument_idx`, `actions_newest_idx`, and foreign keys `ledger_events.correction_of_event_id` and `actions.superseded_by_action_id` (added NOT VALID, validated only if no orphan legacy rows).
- **Case-insensitive email uniqueness.** Emails are lower-cased (rows with a case-variant duplicate are left untouched with a NOTICE) and `users_email_lower_unique` is created unless duplicates remain. Registration and first-run setup store the lower-cased address.
- **Closed vocabularies (0039, `NOT VALID` then validated if clean):** `users.role`, `strategy_instances.status`, `actions.status`, `actions.action_type`, `ledger_events.event_type`, `notification_deliveries.status`, `subscriptions.cadence`, `strategy_versions.lifecycle_status`. `subscriptions.status` is deliberately unconstrained (it mirrors Stripe and app-store states). A new literal in any of these columns needs a new migration that widens the check.
- **Migration runner.** `scripts/migrate.ts` holds a session advisory lock for the whole run, sets `lock_timeout` 30s per migration transaction, warns when an applied migration file is missing from disk, and hashes CRLF-normalised text (recorded legacy raw-byte checksums are still accepted). `.gitattributes` forces `*.sql` to LF.
- **Schema tooling.** `db/migrations/*.sql` is the only schema. The partial informational `src/db/schema.ts`, `drizzle.config.ts`, `drizzle-orm` and `drizzle-kit` were removed on 2026-10-16 (nothing imported them).
- Evidence: `tests/db/integrity-guards.test.ts`, `tests/migration-runner.test.ts`. Not executed in the authoring environment (no Node); CI is the first run.

## Database pool deadlock and client timeouts
- **Pool self-deadlock fixed.** `buildActionCalculation` (and `loadTrustedHistory`) ran every query on the global pool even when called from inside the `sql.begin` transaction in `calculateAction` / `executeAction`, so ten concurrent calls on the `max: 10` pool each held a connection and waited for a second one forever, and the reads also fell outside the locked transaction's snapshot. Both now take a `db: Pick<Sql,"unsafe">` handle; `calculateAction` and `executeAction` pass `tx`, previews and `isStoredActionCurrent` pass the pool. Lock order is unchanged (strategy row, advisory lock, action row).
- **Client timeouts** (`src/lib/db-config.ts`, applied in `src/lib/db.ts`): `connect_timeout` 10 s, `statement_timeout` 60 s, `lock_timeout` 30 s, `idle_in_transaction_session_timeout` 60 s. Code constants, not registry settings: the client is created before the encrypted settings can be read. Migrations, seed, role provisioning and the restore drill use their own clients and are not subject to them.
- **Type handlers explicit.** Date/time/timestamp(tz) are returned as raw text and json/jsonb parameters are passed through untouched via `configureTypeHandlers`, no longer a side effect of constructing an ORM client (the unused `db` export was removed). `drizzle-kit` and `src/db/schema.ts` are untouched.
- **Account export** is sequential, rate limited (5 per hour per user), capped at 50,000 rows per table (truncated tables are named in `truncatedTables`) and sent `cache-control: no-store`.
- **First calculation** after strategy creation now goes through `recalculateAfterMutation`, so a failure marks the strategy NEEDS_ATTENTION and writes an audit event instead of leaving it HEALTHY with no action.
- Evidence: `tests/db-pool-safety.test.ts`, `tests/db/pool-deadlock.test.ts` (8 concurrent calculations on a pool of 3), `tests/db/date-parameters.test.ts`.
- Not executed: this change was authored without a Node toolchain, so typecheck, lint and the tests above have not been run; CI is the first run. The explicit type handlers reproduce, from memory, what the pinned drizzle-orm 0.45.3 postgres-js adapter installed; `tests/db/date-parameters.test.ts` is the check that they match.

## Quarterly target family (branch feat/sig-family-live, 15 October 2026)

- **New engine `QUARTERLY_SIGNAL_TARGET`** replaces the research-only `NINE_SIG_COMMUNITY` engine (deleted together with `sig-calendar-quarter.ts`, which stored a trading-day calendar in the immutable config). Customer-publishable.
- **Three neutral Wealtharr models**, complete under `isMethodComplete`: `quarterly-target-nasdaq-3x` (9% line, 2 skipped sells), `quarterly-target-midcap-2x` (6%, 1 skip), `quarterly-target-smallcap` (3%, 4 skips). All start 60/40 against `US_AGGREGATE_BONDS`. Seeded DISABLED with a PUBLISHED v1.0; they reach customers only after an administrator enables them AND an EXACT market mapping exists for both exposures. No mapping is seeded.
- **Rules implemented (owner decisions, 2026-10-15):** quarter end = last NYSE trading day (rules computed in `sig-nyse-quarters.ts`, close at 16:00 New York); 3-trading-day act window, then the quarter is skipped with one growth step later and new cash carried; first review at the very next quarter end; target anchored on the confirmed actual risk-exposure value; half of new money (contributions, dividends, distributions, interest) lifts the line; 90% purchase throttle on the opening reserve; one single-leg order per recalculation (sell bonds, then buy risk exposure; idle cash swept into bonds); 30 Down on the risk exposure's own closes, normal trading resumes once a window is spent and re-arms only after a close above the line; spike reset (single 2018 source) kept as a labelled choice.

- **Still OPEN (owner/operator):** licensed price history for the risk exposures (nine quarter-end closes); exact UK/US instrument mappings with evidence; enabling in Admin; legal review of the 9Sig-style product text; removal of the legacy unreleased `9sig` plugin (kept for existing ledgers and the CI fixture); Income Sig deliberately not released. Order sizing during the act window uses the latest verified quote, not the quarter-end close. The spike reset is rarely reachable by design of its source condition.

### Additional confirmed P1 fixes on PR #133 (16 October 2026)

- **Quarterly purchase cap drift after a confirmed fill:** `quarterly-signal-engine.ts` previously reverse-engineered the opening reserve from CURRENT market values and fill cash flows; a later bond price change changed the supposed original 90% cap. Now a new SIGNAL review freezes `sigReviewOpeningReserve` in the saved in-review state (without changing historical versions). Later buys use that immutable snapshot minus confirmed buy gross. An older unfinished review with fills but no provable snapshot now returns DATA_REQUIRED for reconciliation rather than inventing an opening reserve. Independent cases in `tests/quarterly-signal-engine.test.ts` assert 4,000 × 90% − 1,000 = 2,600 even after bond price changes.
- **False intraday spike-reset signal:** `quarterly-signal-engine.ts` previously accepted today's price-history row as a completed NYSE close before 16:00 America/New_York. A same-session row is now excluded until the regular close. `tests/quarterly-signal-engine.test.ts` checks 11:00 versus 17:00 New York with a 100→200 observation.
- **Quarterly review prematurely closed following partial funding sales:** `quarterly-signal-engine.ts` previously attempted at most one safe-sleeve sale per review. A confirmed partial bond sale left bond holdings available but could cause a second calculation to close the review with an underfunded risk target. It now repeats bond funding against actual holdings until the buy is funded or the shared execution constraints declare a genuine untradable residue. `tests/quarterly-signal-engine.test.ts` independently checks a 100-of-540 first fill, an additional 440 sale, a 540 risk buy and final HOLD at confirmed 6,540 holdings.
- **Concurrent Stripe replay reclaims:** `src/app/api/stripe/webhook/route.ts` previously used SELECT then an unconditional UPDATE to retake a stale PROCESSING event. Two deliveries could both claim and repeat side effects. Event claim and stale lease recovery now use a single conflict-update with a conditional row lock; `tests/db/billing-lifecycle.test.ts` concurrently re-delivers one signed, deliberately stale event and asserts one cancellation.
- **Verification status:** committed to the existing PR, not merged. The exact final commit still requires full CI green. No production credentials or live payment/broker calls have been used for these tests; real provider authorization/market evidence remain operator gates.

### Quarterly transaction-fee regression (PR #135, 16 October 2026)

- **Fee-induced repeat funding:** With a 540 risk-purchase gap and a 2 flat fee, a 540 bond sale generated 538 net cash. The engine then proposed a 2 sale that generated no proceeds. `quarterly-signal-engine.ts` now sizes the first safe-sleeve sale to 544 (540 risk purchase plus fees on both trades) from the existing execution preference supplied by `action-service.ts`. Actual broker fills, not proposals, advance state. Immutable strategy rules are unchanged.
- **Unexpected actual fee:** A confirmed zero-net safe-sleeve sale caused by actual fees exceeding the configured estimate now blocks the next funding sale as DATA_REQUIRED until reconciled. `sig-confirmed-ledger.ts` reads the latest chronological unreversed safe-sleeve fill.
- **Regression evidence:** `tests/quarterly-signal-engine.test.ts` independently checks 544 sale minus 2 fee = 542 cash; 540 risk buy plus 2 fee = zero residual cash; and that a zero-net unexpected fee fails closed. `tests/db/quarterly-target-gbp-review.test.ts` verifies two actual GBP fees and a preserved 100 cash reserve.
- **Operator verification:** The flat fee is an estimate until execution. Percentage-based or unconfigured broker fees can require reconciliation. No provider charges are invented or automatically attributed.

### Stripe subscription item integrity (follow-up after PR #135, 16 October 2026)

- **P1 mis-entitlement risk:** `stripe-subscription-state.ts` used `canonical.items.data[0]` without verifying the entire live Stripe subscription. A two-price subscription could silently grant the first plan, quantity >1 could silently charge for multiple copies, and an unusual recurring interval (weekly or every three months) was treated as monthly/annual. These do not match Wealtharr's one-price, one-unit, monthly/annual product model.
- **Fixed:** Only an exactly one-item, one-unit, one-month-or-year Stripe subscription may update a customer's entitlement. An unknown or unsupported live Stripe state now fails retryably with an explicit diagnostic code; ownership, immutable financial histories and existing local subscriptions are not modified. Terminated subscriptions still reconcile normally.
- **Independent regression:** `tests/db/billing-lifecycle.test.ts` feeds signed, canonically retrieved mock Stripe subscriptions with two mapped prices, quantity 2, weekly cadence and three-month interval through the real handler and PostgreSQL. Each must preserve the prior plan, cadence, Stripe subscription ID and current period end while rejecting the incompatible state.
