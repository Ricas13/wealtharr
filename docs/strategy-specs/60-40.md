# Global 60/40 (GBP) model

**Status: a named Wealtharr model, not one author's strategy.** The 60/40 stock/bond split is a widely
used allocation; no single author owns a precise, complete 60/40 rule set. This card therefore defines a
Wealtharr model in exact terms. Every statement below is labelled **SOURCE** (stated by the cited source)
or **CONVENTION** (chosen by Wealtharr, to be confirmed by the owner). Nothing here is investment advice,
a forecast or a performance claim.

Engine: `FIXED_ALLOCATION`. Catalogue key: `60-40`. Plugin: `src/domain/strategy/catalog/60-40.ts`.

## Source

- Bogleheads wiki, "Asset allocation": https://www.bogleheads.org/wiki/Asset_allocation
- SOURCE: describes dividing a portfolio between stocks and bonds, and gives 60/40 as a commonly used
  split. It names asset classes only. It does not define a global equity index, a bond index, a currency
  hedge, a maturity band, a rebalancing schedule or a threshold.
- Primary-source re-verification of the wiki text by a person is still outstanding (this card was written
  without a re-read of the live page). Treat the SOURCE labels as "believed, to be re-checked".

### Exposure definitions (CONVENTION)

Registered in `src/domain/strategy/exposures.ts` with structured descriptors.

- `GLOBAL_EQUITY_ALL_CAP_UNHEDGED`: equity; region GLOBAL (developed and emerging markets);
  all capitalisations (large, mid and small); currency policy UNHEDGED (underlying currencies are held,
  not hedged to sterling); leverage 1; maturity not applicable.
- `GLOBAL_AGGREGATE_BONDS_GBP_HEDGED`: bonds; region GLOBAL; investment-grade government,
  government-related and corporate bonds; all maturities (no short, intermediate or long restriction);
  currency policy GBP_HEDGED (hedged to sterling); leverage 1.

The older exposures `BROAD_EQUITY` and `AGGREGATE_BONDS` are deliberately vague and are not used by this
model. Other models that still use them are specified separately.

Why these choices (reasoning, not a source claim): a UK investor who holds one global equity fund is
exposed to currency movements through its holdings; hedging the bond leg to sterling is a common way to
keep the bond leg's role as the lower-volatility ballast. This is a convention and the owner may prefer
an unhedged or a UK-government bond leg; that would be a separate named model with its own exposures.

## Rebalancing rule (exact)

Notation: `V_E`, `V_B` current values of the equity and bond legs, `C` cash held in the strategy,
`T = V_E + V_B + C` (the total). Target values are `0.60 * T` and `0.40 * T`.

1. **No rule applies between reviews.** The engine does nothing until a review is due
   (action `NO_ACTION`, "No allocation review is due yet").
2. **At a review** the engine compares each leg with its target. With threshold 0.00 the portfolio
   holds only if both legs are exactly on target to the unit the engine works in; any other drift
   triggers a rebalance.
3. **Cash is used first.** If cash is held and a leg is underweight, the engine proposes buying the most
   underweight leg with available cash, up to the cash held, before proposing any sale.
4. **Otherwise the overweight leg is sold**, and the proceeds fund the purchase of the underweight leg;
   the next step is recalculated from the actual fill. The full plan (sales first, then purchases, amounts
   rounded to the penny with the final purchase absorbing the rounding residue) is shown with the action.
5. **The review closes only on a HOLD**, i.e. when a recalculation after the fills finds the portfolio
   within the threshold.

Holdings outside the two modelled exposures stop the calculation (`DATA_REQUIRED`); the engine never
guesses a classification.

## Cash flows and costs (what the model does and does not define)

- **Contributions (deposits).** Cash added to the strategy is part of `T` at the next calculation and is
  used to buy the underweight leg first (rule 3). There is no separate contribution rule and no
  contribution-timed rebalance: contributions are invested at a review, not on arrival.
  CONVENTION.
- **Withdrawals.** A withdrawal is a ledger event that lowers cash or holdings. The model has no
  withdrawal rule: the next review recalculates from the reduced holdings and rebalances to 60/40. If cash
  is insufficient the investor records the sale they made; the engine never assumes a negative cash
  balance. CONVENTION.
- **Dividends and distributions.** Cash distributions are recorded as cash in the strategy and are
  handled exactly as a contribution. Accumulating funds need no handling. CONVENTION.
- **Fees and taxes.** Broker fees, fund charges, spreads and stamp duty are not part of the target
  calculation. A recorded fee reduces cash/holdings and is reflected at the next review. Inside a UK ISA
  there is no income or capital gains tax to model. CONVENTION.
- **Minimum trade, whole shares, cash buffer.** These are per-account execution constraints of the
  investor's broker (`execution_constraints`), not part of the model. They can make an amount
  impractical; the engine then falls back to another leg or reports why it cannot proceed.
- **Execution residue.** When whole shares, a minimum trade or a cash buffer leave drift that no
  practical order can remove, the review would otherwise never close at threshold 0. A separate
  engine change (see the "Execution-residue closure" section of `docs/strategy-engines.md`, when that
  change is merged) closes such a review with a recorded HOLD. That is a Wealtharr implementation
  convention, not part of this strategy. Until it is merged, such a review stays at `DATA_REQUIRED`.

## Review dates (what the engine does today)

- The first review is due 12 months after the strategy was started (calendar date in the strategy's
  time zone, falling back to the investor's time zone), rolled to the previous business day, at the local
  cut-off 16:00.
- After a review closes on a HOLD, the next review is measured **from the instant the HOLD was
  confirmed**, not from the original anniversary. The review date therefore drifts later by however long
  each review took to complete. **Known drift:** the model is not "rebalance on each anniversary of the
  start date"; making it so would be an engine change and a new version.
- Weekends are non-business days; market holidays are only applied if a holiday list is configured, and
  none is configured for this model. Treat review dates as approximate to within a business day or two.

## Instrument requirements for a verified listing (GB / ISA / GBP)

No ISINs are named here; the mappings are operator data (`regional_instrument_mappings`, fidelity
`EXACT`) and are never seeded. A listing may be verified for a leg only when all of the following hold.

Both legs:
- Tradable in GBP on a UK-accessible venue (a GBP line, or a line whose trade currency matches the
  account currency; no silent foreign-currency substitution).
- ISA-eligible for the investor's broker and account type.
- Unleveraged (leverage exactly 1), long-only, not inverse, not synthetic-leverage.
- A single fund per leg; no basket of funds is accepted as one leg.
- Current price from the configured data source, with a fresh timestamp.
- Documented total ongoing charge and tracking basis, recorded with the mapping.

Equity leg (`GLOBAL_EQUITY_ALL_CAP_UNHEDGED`): tracks one global all-capitalisation index covering
developed and emerging markets; share class is unhedged.

Bond leg (`GLOBAL_AGGREGATE_BONDS_GBP_HEDGED`): tracks one global investment-grade aggregate bond index
of all maturities; the share class is hedged to sterling.

A fund that is "global developed only", "developed plus emerging, large and mid only" or a single-region
or single-maturity bond fund does **not** satisfy these definitions; it would need a separately named
model or an owner-approved widening of the definition. Whether a close-but-not-identical fund is
acceptable is an owner decision (below), never an automatic substitution.
